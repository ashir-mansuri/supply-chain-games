Supply Chain Games

Nine classroom games about the decisions that keep goods moving. Three team games, played in class or online with each student on their own phone or laptop, and six solo games for practice or homework, four of them about doing business across borders. Nothing to install.

Dock 1

Supplier Showdown

Buy four parts for an electric scooter maker in Sanand from suppliers in China, Vietnam, India and Germany. Fires, floods, tariffs and chip shortages hit over eight months. Cheap or safe: which one wins?

Players
Teams of 3 to 5, each member on their own device
Time
About 30 minutes of play, plus debrief
Topics
Supplier selection, total cost of ownership, dual sourcing, buffer stock, supply risk
Play Supplier Showdown
Dock 2

Warehouse Rush

Pick customer orders on a warehouse map, then use last month’s sales to move fast sellers next to dispatch, then try batch picking. The same six orders, three times, getting faster each time.

Players
Teams of 2 to 4, taking turns as the picker
Time
About 20 minutes of play, plus debrief
Topics
ABC analysis, Pareto principle, slotting, pick routes, batch picking, productivity
Play Warehouse Rush
Dock 3

Coffee Chain

Run one stage of a coffee supply chain, from a roaster in Bengaluru to a café in Vadodara. Order week by week, see only your own stage, and find out what one small change in demand does further up the chain. Modelled on the beer game.

Players
Teams of 4, one stage each, or solo against the computer
Time
About 25 minutes of play, plus debrief
Topics
Bullwhip effect, lead times, information sharing, stock versus backlog costs
Play Coffee Chain
Dock 4

Boundary Bakes Solo

Run a bakery stall outside a cricket stadium during a 10-day tournament. Each morning, decide how many brownies and sandwiches to make. Too few and fans walk away; too many and the leftovers lose money.

Players
One student, on any phone or laptop
Time
About 15 minutes, plus debrief
Topics
Perishable stock, uncertain demand, cost of too many versus too few, critical ratio
Play Boundary Bakes
Dock 5

Season Planner Solo

Plan six months of production at a school uniform factory in Surat, with a big peak before schools reopen. Hire, run overtime, subcontract or build stock early, and meet the orders at the lowest cost.

Players
One student, on any phone or laptop
Time
About 20 minutes, plus debrief
Topics
Aggregate planning, level versus chase strategy, capacity, overtime, subcontracting
Play Season Planner
Dock 6

Export Desk Solo

Ship a Rajkot pump maker’s orders to Germany, the USA, the UAE and Kenya. Choose sea, sea-air or air for each order, decide whether to lock the exchange rate, and whether to insure the cargo, while canal blockages, port strikes and currency swings hit along the way.

Players
One student, on any phone or laptop
Time
About 15 minutes, plus debrief
Topics
International logistics, transit time versus cost, currency risk and forward contracts, cargo insurance
Play Export Desk
Dock 7

Go Global Solo

Take an Ayurvedic skincare brand from Ahmedabad into the UAE, UK, USA, Germany and Kenya over three years. In each market, choose an online store, a distributor, a joint venture or your own subsidiary, and face tariffs, currency crashes and partner disputes.

Players
One student, on any phone or laptop
Time
About 20 minutes, plus debrief
Topics
Market selection, entry modes, step-by-step internationalisation, country risk
Play Go Global
Dock 8

Factory Footprint Solo

A tile maker in Morbi sells in India, the Gulf, Europe and the USA. Over six years, decide where to build plants: at home, in the UAE, Poland or Mexico. Compare landed costs as a US duty, a European carbon fee, a freight spike and a gas cut change the picture.

Players
One student, on any phone or laptop
Time
About 20 minutes, plus debrief
Topics
Global facility location, landed cost, tariffs and carbon border fees, capacity planning, concentration risk
Play Factory Footprint
Dock 9

Freight Buyer Solo

Buy container space for a Tiruppur textile exporter shipping to Rotterdam for two years. Each quarter, book space on contract with a large or a small shipping line, or leave it to the spot market, while freight rates calm, spike and crash.

Players
One student, on any phone or laptop
Time
About 15 minutes, plus debrief
Topics
Freight procurement, contract versus spot, carrier reliability, price volatility
Play Freight Buyer
