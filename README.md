# Supply Chain Games

Nine free games for supply chain and operations management. There are three team games, played by a team gathered round one phone or laptop, and six solo games, four of them about doing business across borders. Nothing to install.

**Play:** https://ashir-mansuri.github.io/supply-chain-games/

Created by Ashir Mansuri. Free to use and adapt with credit, under the [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) licence.

| Game | Play time | What you learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Supplier Showdown** | About 30 minutes | Supplier selection, total cost of ownership, dual sourcing, buffer stock, supply risk |
| **Warehouse Rush** | About 20 minutes | Pareto principle, ABC analysis, slotting, pick routes, batch picking, picking productivity |
| **Coffee Chain** | About 25 minutes | Bullwhip effect, lead times, information sharing, stock versus backlog costs (modelled on the beer game) |
| **Boundary Bakes** (solo) | About 15 minutes | Perishable stock, uncertain demand, cost of too many versus too few, critical ratio |
| **Season Planner** (solo) | About 20 minutes | Aggregate planning, level versus chase strategy, overtime, subcontracting, building stock |
| **Export Desk** (solo) | About 15 minutes | International shipping, transit time versus cost, currency risk and forward contracts, cargo insurance |
| **Go Global** (solo) | About 20 minutes | Choosing foreign markets, entry modes (online, distributor, joint venture, subsidiary), step-by-step expansion, country risk |
| **Factory Footprint** (solo) | About 20 minutes | Global plant location, landed cost, tariffs and carbon border fees, capacity planning, concentration risk |
| **Freight Buyer** (solo) | About 15 minutes | Buying freight, contract versus spot rates, carrier reliability, price swings |

## India and Europe versions

Every page has a switch at the top right. **India** is the original version, in English, with prices in rupees. **Europe** sets the same games in Europe, with prices in euros, and comes in **English** and **Deutsch** (German). The rules, decisions and lessons are the same in every version; only the places, companies, events and currency change.

| Game | India setting | Europe setting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier Showdown | Electric scooter maker in Sanand; suppliers in China, Vietnam, Pune and Stuttgart | E-bike maker in Leipzig; suppliers in China, Vietnam, Wrocław (Poland) and Taichung (Taiwan) |
| Warehouse Rush | Festive sale at a fulfilment centre near Ahmedabad | Black Friday week at a fulfilment centre near Leipzig |
| Coffee Chain | Roaster in Bengaluru to a café in Vadodara | Roaster in Hamburg to a café in Munich |
| Boundary Bakes / **Halftime Bakes** | Bakery stall at a cricket stadium | Bakery stall at a football stadium in Munich (the game is called Halftime Bakes in Europe) |
| Season Planner | School uniform factory in Surat | Garden furniture maker in Bielefeld |
| Export Desk | Rajkot pump maker shipping to Germany, the USA, the UAE and Kenya | Dortmund pump maker shipping to India, China, the UK and Nigeria |
| Go Global | Ayurvedic skincare brand from Ahmedabad | Natural skincare brand from Freiburg, entering the UAE, UK, USA, Japan and Kenya |
| Factory Footprint | Tile maker in Morbi; plants at home, in the UAE, Poland or Mexico | Tile maker in Castellón, Spain; plants at home, in the UAE, the UK or Mexico |
| Freight Buyer | Tiruppur textile exporter shipping to Rotterdam | Hamburg homeware importer bringing goods from Shanghai |

Each version saves its own progress, so switching version doesn't overwrite a game in progress. Players who want to compare results should all use the same version and the same game code.

## How to play

### Starting a game

Every game runs in a web browser on a phone, tablet or laptop. Nothing to install or sign in to.

1. Open the game from the home page.
2. Type your name, or your team's name if you're playing as a team round one screen.
3. Keep the game code shown, or type a code you've agreed with others. Players with the same code get the same events, so results can be compared.
4. Tap **Start the game** (**Spiel starten** in German).
5. Every game except Warehouse Rush saves your progress on the device. If you leave, open the game again and tap **Continue** on the start page.

In Coffee Chain you also choose whether to play one stage against the computer, or all four stages on one screen.

At the end of every game, tap **Copy my result** to copy a one-line summary you can share.

### Supplier Showdown

**Your job:** You run purchasing at Sabar Scooters, an electric scooter maker in Sanand. Every scooter needs four parts: a **battery pack**, a **hub motor**, a **controller chip** and a **tyre set**. Each part can come from four suppliers: **Shenzhen** (China), **Hanoi** (Vietnam), **Pune** (India) and **Stuttgart** (Germany). The game runs for 8 months, April to November.

**Goal:** Finish November with the highest total profit.

**Where you start:** The previous manager bought everything from Shenzhen because it was cheapest.

#### Each month

1. **Read the plan screen.**
   - *Dealers expect to order:* the forecast number of scooters. Actual orders can be a few percent higher or lower.
   - *News desk:* warnings about this month, problems still going on, price or duty changes, and late shipments arriving.
   - *In stock:* how many of each part are left in your store.

2. **Split each part's order between suppliers.**
   - Each supplier row shows the **quoted price**, the **landed cost** (price plus import duty and freight), the **lead time**, the **defect rate** and the **risk**.
   - Tap **+** or **−** to move the share by 10% at a time. Each part must total **100%**.
   - Pune can make at most **60%** of your battery packs and **40%** of your chips.
   - A supplier marked **New supplier** costs **₹5 lakh** to approve and ships only **half** your order in its first month.
   - The line under each part shows how many you will order: forecast + buffer − stock.

3. **Choose your buffer stock:** None, 1 week, 2 weeks or 4 weeks of forecast demand. Buffer stock protects you from shortages but costs 2% of its value every month.

4. **Check the bar at the bottom.** It shows your parts cost per scooter (quoted and landed) and any new-supplier fees this month.

5. **Place your orders:** tap **Place orders for [month]**.

6. **Read the news card.** Something happens every month: a fire, a flood, a strike, a tariff, a chip shortage, a quality problem, the festive rush, or a quiet month. Tap **See [month] results**.

7. **Read your results.**
   - Dealers ordered, scooters built, lost sales and profit for the month.
   - The parts table shows, for each part: ordered, arrived, late (arriving next month), not shipped, faulty and left in store.
   - A scooter needs all four parts, so **the scarcest part limits how many you build**. The part that ran out is marked in red.
   - The profit and loss table shows where the money went.

8. Tap **Plan [next month]** and repeat.

#### At the end

After November, tap **See the final report**. It shows:
- Total profit, orders delivered, parts cost per scooter, suppliers per part, and shipping carbon per scooter.
- A chart of your profit month by month against three strategies the computer played with the same news: **Cheapest only**, **Local first**, and **Two suppliers + buffer**.
- The news for each month and your hardest month.
- Questions to talk over with your team.

#### Tips
- Compare **landed cost**, not just quoted price.
- Read the news desk every month. Some problems come with a warning.
- A new supplier needs a month to reach full deliveries. Think ahead.

### Warehouse Rush

**Your job:** You pick customer orders at a fulfilment centre near Ahmedabad during the festive sale.

**Goal:** Pick the same six customer orders faster in each round. Your score is **lines picked per hour**.

#### Reading the map
- Four aisles, **A** to **D**. **Dispatch** is at the bottom; every trip starts and ends there.
- Each shelf shows a product's SKU number. A slot name like **B3L** means aisle B, bay 3 counting from the dispatch end, left side.
- **Gold** shelves are on your pick list. **Green** shelves are picked.
- The **dark circle with a gold ring** is the picker. The **blue line** is the path walked.
- The picker can only walk along aisles and the cross-aisles at the front and back.

#### How time is counted
- Each trip takes **20 seconds** to set up.
- Each line picked takes **10 seconds**.
- Walking speed is **1.2 metres per second**.

#### Round 1: Day one (products scattered at random)
1. Tap **Start round 1**.
2. Read the pick list on the right: SKU, product name and slot.
3. Tap the gold shelves **in the order you want to visit them**. The picker walks your route.
4. After the last shelf, the picker returns to dispatch. The **Trip done** panel shows the metres you walked, the shortest possible route, your route efficiency and the picker time. Tap **Show the shortest route** to see the best route drawn in red dashes.
5. Tap **Next order**. There are 6 orders in the round.
6. The round results show lines per hour, metres walked, picker time and route efficiency.

#### Round 2: ABC slotting (move the fast sellers)
1. Tap **Next: study the sales data and re-slot the warehouse**.
2. Study the chart of last month's sales.
   - **A** = the top 7 products, which make about 67% of all picks.
   - **B** = the next 11 products, about 19% of picks.
   - **C** = the last 18 products, about 14% of picks.
3. Re-slot the warehouse. Tap a shelf to pick it up, then tap another slot; the two swap places.
   - **Dark blue** shelves are A products. **Light blue** shelves are B products.
   - The **fast-pick zone** is the 8 slots nearest dispatch, marked with gold dashed outlines.
   - Move all 7 A products into the fast-pick zone, with B products close behind. Watch **Walk per pick now** drop.
   - **Do it for me** shows the best layout. **Undo all moves** goes back to the day-one layout.
4. Tap **Pick with this layout** and pick the same six orders again.

#### Round 3: Batch picking (two orders per trip)
1. Tap **Next: batch picking**, then **Start round 3**.
2. Each trip now has two orders, one in **Tote 1** and one in **Tote 2**. You make 3 trips instead of 6.
3. Pick them the same way as before.

#### At the end
Tap **See the final results**. You see how much faster you got, your lines per hour in each round, metres walked compared with the shortest possible, and questions to talk over.

### Coffee Chain

**Your job:** You run one stage of a coffee supply chain. Roasted coffee travels down the chain, and orders travel up it:

**Coffee estates → Coffee roaster (Bengaluru) → Wholesaler (Ahmedabad) → Distributor (Vadodara) → Café (Vadodara) → Customers**

**Goal:** The lowest **total cost for the whole team** (all four stages added together) by the end of the game.

**Costs, for every stage, every week:**
- **₹10** for each bag in stock.
- **₹20** for each bag you owe the stage below you (backlog).

**Delays:**
- An order takes **1 week** to reach the stage above you.
- Coffee takes **2 weeks** to travel down one step.

#### Each week
1. **Read your stage's screen.** It shows:
   - *Bags in stock*, *Owed to* the stage below you, and *Your cost so far*.
   - *Arrived* this week from the stage above you.
   - *New order* this week from the stage below you. At the café, this is what customers want.
   - *Sent* this week. If you didn't have enough, the rest is added to what you owe and goes out when stock arrives.
   - *On the way to you:* bags arriving next week and in two weeks, and how much the stage above still owes you.
   - A table of your last five weeks.
2. **Decide your order.** Type how many bags to order from the stage above, or use **−10** and **+10**.
3. **Send it.**
   - *Solo:* tap **Send my order**. The computer orders for the other three stages.
   - *All four stages on one device:* set all four orders, then tap **Send all four orders**.
4. The next week starts automatically. The game doesn't say how many weeks are left.

**Information sharing:** if you tick it on the start page, every stage also sees the café's customer sales each week.

#### At the end
The final report shows:
- Your team's total cost, and how it compares with three computer teams: **Typical players**, **Pass the order on** and **Plan from customer sales**.
- **The secret:** what customers really bought each week.
- A chart of every stage's orders, week by week, against real customer sales. This shows the bullwhip effect.
- A chart of each stage's stock, and what it owed.
- Each stage's cost, its order swing, its biggest order and the number of weeks it owed bags.
- Questions to talk over.

#### Tips
- Before you order, add up what is already on the way to you.
- A big order from the stage below doesn't always mean customers are buying more.

### Boundary Bakes (solo)

**Your job:** You run a bakery stall outside a cricket stadium in Ahmedabad during a 10-day T20 tournament. Every morning, before the gates open, you decide how many **brownie boxes** and **fresh sandwiches** to make.

**Goal:** The highest total profit by the end of the final.

| Product | Costs you | You sell it for | If it doesn't sell that day |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brownie box | ₹120 | ₹260 | Sold next morning to a café for ₹60 |
| Fresh sandwich | ₹80 | ₹100 | Thrown away (worth ₹0) |

#### How to play
1. Open **Boundary Bakes**, type your name, keep or type a game code, then tap **Start the game**.
2. **Read today's forecast** for each product. It shows the expected number of customers and the range they usually fall in ("on two days out of three, between … and …"). Crowds grow as the tournament heads for the final.
3. **Decide how many to make** of each product. Type a number or use **−5** and **+5**. The cost of making them is shown beside the number.
4. Tap **Open the stall**.
5. **Read the results:** how many fans wanted each product, how many you sold, how many were left over, how many fans you turned away, and your profit for the day.
6. Tap **Next day** and repeat for all 10 days.

#### At the end
The final report shows your profit and the best possible profit. It compares you with **Make the forecast**, **Critical ratio** and **Perfect foresight**, charts what you made against what fans wanted, and explains the critical ratio. Tap **Copy my result** to copy a one-line summary.

**Tip:** Compare what one too few costs you with what one too many costs you, separately for each product.

### Season Planner (solo)

**Your job:** You run production at a garment unit in Surat that makes school uniform sets. Orders from shops grow from January to a big peak in May, before schools reopen, then drop in June.

**Goal:** Meet the orders at the **lowest total cost** for the season.

**Your levers each month:**
- **Workers:** each makes 400 sets a month and costs ₹16,000 a month. Hiring costs ₹10,000 per worker (up to 20 a month); a layoff costs ₹15,000.
- **Overtime:** none, +10%, +20% or +25% extra output, at ₹60 per set.
- **Subcontracting:** up to 5,000 sets a month from another factory, at ₹90 per set.
- **Stock:** sets made early wait in the store at ₹8 per set per month.

**What can go wrong:** orders you can't fill are lost at ₹150 per set. Actual orders can be up to 8% above or below the forecast. Sets left after June are cleared at a loss of ₹50 each.

#### How to play
1. Open **Season Planner**, type your name and the game code, then tap **Start the game**.
2. **Look at the forecast chart** for all six months before you decide. The gold bar is this month. You start with 20 workers and 2,000 sets in stock.
3. **Set this month's plan:** workers (**−5**, **−1**, **+1**, **+5**), overtime, and subcontracting (in steps of 250 or 1,000).
4. **Check the preview.** It shows what you'll make, the stock you'll have left (or how many you'll be short) if orders match the forecast, and the expected cost for the month.
5. Tap **Run [month]**. You then see the actual orders, sets made, orders lost, stock at month end and the month's costs.
6. Tap **Plan [next month]** and repeat until June.

#### At the end
The final report shows your total cost and cost per set. A chart shows orders, production and stock. Your plan is compared with a **Level plan**, a **Chase plan** and the **Best mixed plan**, and a table shows where the money went. Tap **Copy my result** to copy a one-line summary.

**Tip:** Build some stock before the peak, but count what storing it costs.

### Export Desk (solo)

**Your job:** You run the export desk at a pump maker in Rajkot. Eight orders arrive from customers in Hamburg (Germany), New York (USA), Dubai (UAE) and Mombasa (Kenya). Each order is priced in euros or US dollars and has a delivery deadline.

**Goal:** The highest total profit, in rupees, across all eight orders.

**Your three decisions for each order:**
1. **How to ship it:** by sea (via the Suez Canal or the longer route round the Cape of Good Hope), sea to Dubai then air, or air. Faster costs more. Each option shows the cost, the usual number of days, the chance of damage, and whether it should arrive on time.
2. **How to handle the currency:** **wait** and convert at whatever the rate is on payment day, or **lock the rate now** with a forward contract (today's rate, less 0.6%).
3. **Whether to insure the cargo:** insurance costs 0.4% of the order value and pays for any damage on the way.

**What can go wrong:** a ship stuck in the Suez Canal, a dock strike in Hamburg, a sudden move in the euro or dollar, damaged cargo, or a shortage of air cargo space that makes air freight 80% dearer. Late delivery costs 1% of the order value for every day late, up to 15%. Some trouble is announced in the **Shipping news** before you book; some isn't.

#### How to play
1. Open **Export Desk**, type your name, keep or type a game code, then tap **Start the game**.
2. **Read the order:** the destination, the value in euros or dollars, what it's worth in rupees today, what the pumps cost to make, and the deadline.
3. **Read the Shipping news** on the right (below on a phone).
4. **Choose a route.** The green or red tag on each route shows how many days to spare, or how late it is likely to be.
5. **Choose how to handle the currency**, and tick **Buy cargo insurance** if you want it.
6. Tap **Ship order [number]**. You then see when the goods arrived, the exchange rate on payment day, whether the cargo was damaged, and the profit on the order.
7. Tap **Next order** and repeat for all eight orders.

#### At the end
The final report shows your total profit, how much went on freight, late penalties and insurance, how many orders arrived on time, and how much you gained or lost on currency. Your results are compared with **Cheapest every time**, **Fastest and safest every time** and **Match the route to the deadline**. Tap **Copy my result** to copy a one-line summary.

**Tip:** Check the deadline before the price. A late penalty can cost more than flying the goods.

### Go Global (solo)

**Your job:** You lead international growth at Sona Herbals, an Ayurvedic skincare brand from Ahmedabad. You have ₹10 crore to take the brand into five markets: the UAE, the UK, the USA, Germany and Kenya.

**Goal:** The highest **company worth** after three years: your cash at the end plus two years of profit at your final rate.

**Four ways into a market:**

| Way in | Buyers reached | Your share of sales | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Online store | 20% | 32% | Cheap to set up and run |
| Distributor | 50% | 15% | Cheap to set up, no running cost |
| Joint venture with a local partner | 70% | 18% | Bigger set-up and running cost; costs money to leave |
| Own subsidiary | 90% | 36% | The most expensive to set up, run and leave |

Set-up costs are higher in markets far from India. A joint venture or subsidiary works best once you know the market: opened with no experience there, it starts slowly and has a 50% chance the product needs reworking. After at least one round in the market (as an online store or with a distributor, say) that chance drops to 10%.

**What can go wrong (or right):** currency falls in Kenya and the UK, a US tariff on imports (a subsidiary that makes locally avoids it), stricter rules on herbal products in Germany, a quarrel with a joint-venture partner, a spending slump in the USA, and a social media trend that boosts sales.

#### How to play
1. Open **Go Global**, type your name, keep or type a game code, then tap **Start the game**.
2. Each round is six months. For **each market**, look at the market size, its growth, and your profit there last round.
3. **Choose how to be in each market:** Not present, Online store, Distributor, Joint venture or Own subsidiary. Each choice shows what it costs to make the change now and about how much profit it earns when fully running.
4. **Check the dark bar at the bottom:** your cash now, what this round's changes cost, and your cash after the changes. You can't spend more than you have.
5. Tap **Run [half-year]**. You then see the world news for those six months, and your sales and profit in each market.
6. Tap **Plan [next half-year]** and repeat for all six rounds.

#### At the end
The final report shows your company worth, a chart of your cash after each round, your path in each market and the world news for your game code. Your result is compared with **Distributors everywhere**, **All in from day one** and **Step by step**. Tap **Copy my result** to copy a one-line summary.

**Tip:** Test a market cheaply before you commit big money to it.

### Factory Footprint (solo)

**Your job:** You run operations at Kiran Ceramics, a tile maker in Morbi with six plants. You sell tiles in India, the Gulf, Europe and the USA, and at the start everything is made in Morbi.

**Goal:** The highest **company worth** after six years: your cash at the end plus two years of profit at your final year's rate. You start with ₹150 crore.

**Your decision each year:** how many plants to have at each of four sites: Morbi (India), Ras Al Khaimah (UAE), Poland and Monterrey (Mexico).
- Each plant makes up to 25 lakh boxes a year and costs ₹6 crore a year to run. Sites outside India also have a yearly site cost.
- A new plant takes a year to build: plants you start this year produce from next year.
- Closing a plant costs ₹15 crore and takes effect at once.

**Landed cost** is what one box costs by the time it reaches the customer: the cost to make it, plus freight, plus import duty, plus any other fees. Tiles are cheapest to make in India, but freight and duties can make another site cheaper for some markets. Each year your plants automatically send their tiles to the markets where they earn the most. Demand you can't meet is lost.

**What can change:** a US duty on tiles from India, a European carbon fee on tiles made outside Europe, a year of double freight rates, a gas cut at one site, and a building boom in India or the Gulf.

#### How to play
1. Open **Factory Footprint**, type your name, keep or type a game code, then tap **Start the game**.
2. **Check capacity and demand:** the gold bar shows what your plants can make, and the black line shows forecast demand. The table shows each market's forecast.
3. **Read the landed cost table.** Green shows the cheapest site for each market, with the duties and fees in force now.
4. **Set the number of plants at each site** with the **−** and **+** buttons. The card shows what the change costs.
5. **Check the dark bar at the bottom:** your cash now, what this year's changes cost, and your cash after them. You can't spend more than you have.
6. Tap **Run [year]**. You then see the world news for that year, where your tiles went, how much demand you couldn't meet, and your profit.
7. Tap **Plan [next year]** and repeat for all six years. In the last year you can only close plants.

#### At the end
The final report shows your company worth, a chart of your cash after each year, your plants at each site and the world news for your game code. Your result is compared with **Stay in India**, **A plant in every region** and **Plants where landed cost gaps are biggest**. Tap **Copy my result** to copy a one-line summary.

**Tip:** A plant takes a year to build. Think about where costs are heading, not just where they are today.

### Freight Buyer (solo)

**Your job:** You buy shipping for Kaveri Home Textiles, a towel and bed-linen exporter in Tiruppur. About 40 containers a quarter go from Chennai to Rotterdam.

**Goal:** Ship every container at the lowest **total cost** over two years (eight quarters). Lower is better.

**Your decision each quarter:** how many containers to book on contract with each of two shipping lines. Anything you don't book goes on the **spot market** at that quarter's rate.

| | Anchor Line | Seabird Line | Spot market |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rate | Fixed before the quarter | About 7% lower than Anchor | The going rate, known only afterwards |
| Normal market | Keeps all your booking | Keeps all your booking | Plenty of space |
| Tight market | Keeps all your booking | Keeps half | Space for 40% of your need |
| Ships full | Keeps 85% | Keeps 30% | Space for 10% of your need |

**What it costs:**
- Contract rates follow the market slowly, so they're below the spot rate when rates are rising and above it when rates are falling.
- If you book more than you need, you pay half the rate for each empty slot.
- Containers that can't ship wait for next quarter and cost ₹2 lakh each for every quarter they wait.

#### How to play
1. Open **Freight Buyer**, type your name, keep or type a game code, then tap **Start the game**.
2. **Check this quarter:** the forecast number of containers (the real number can be 15% higher or lower), any containers still waiting, last quarter's spot rate and the shipping news.
3. **Look at the rate chart** to see how spot and contract rates have moved.
4. **Book containers** with Anchor Line and Seabird Line: type a number or use **−5** and **+5**. Your bookings carry over to the next quarter, so you only need to change them.
5. Tap **Book and ship**. You then see the spot rate, any bookings the lines cancelled, how many containers went on spot, how many were left waiting, and the cost.
6. Tap **Plan [next quarter]** and repeat for all eight quarters.

#### At the end
The final report shows your total cost and cost per container, charts of the rates and your cost over time, and every quarter's results. Your result is compared with **Spot only**, **Cheapest contract** and **Reliable base, spot top-up**. Tap **Copy my result** to copy a one-line summary.

**Tip:** A cheap contract is only worth something if the shipping line keeps it when space runs short.
